# Connected Pantheon: Shared History

This is an author-facing, spoiler-bearing summary of the shared history in `docs/lore-dawn.md`, `docs/lore-elements.md`, `docs/lore-mortal.md` and their checked-in chronicle/relationship resources. It is not a new source of quest requirements. The existing catalog remains authoritative for titles, branch keys, deeds, blessings and numerical rewards.

## The Concord And Sundering

The First Concord genuinely protected mortal life from oblivion. It was not secretly a plan to exploit worshippers, nor a promise to abolish natural death. Its Sundering confronted the gods with rescue duties, dangerous alternatives and concealed sacrifices. No single hidden villain explains the catastrophe. A successful rescue and a harmful concealment can both be true; one does not cancel the other.

Chronicles distinguish established events from what a god believed, claimed or omitted. Branches change how a god confronts that history and what the player practices now. They do not produce alternate historical facts, resurrect every victim, reverse the Sundering or certify a painless option that nobody chose.

## Interlocking Accounts

| Connection | Shared Historical Anchor | Belief Or Uncertainty To Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solarius and Nythea | Solarius sealed the breach believing Nythea had abandoned the defenders. Nythea sheltered survivors in dreams and could not return them all. | The abandonment accusation is mistaken, not canon. Nythea's assurance of temporary sleep hid uncertainty; not every missing sleeper is established dead or recoverable. Present phantoms are not identified as those sleepers. |
| Aethon and Kairoth | Ancient brothers: Aethon foresaw the catastrophe; Kairoth withheld a prevention timeline whose cost was erasing innocent lives. He did not enact it. | A true warning was not knowledge of every alternative. Refusing erasure does not excuse concealing the decision. Elaris disputes gaps in the chronology; the gap proves neither a harmless remedy nor that erasure occurred. |
| Nashara, Elaris, Dravak, Varun and Nythea | Former confidants Nashara and Elaris forged a retreat order and concealed its proof, respectively, to support the refugee rescue. Dravak held the line under that order while Varun evacuated his pack; Nythea hid the refugees. | Saving refugees did not obtain Dravak's informed consent. The forgery is not proof of one omnipotent conspiracy; concealed evidence does not erase the rescue or its costs. Protecting Nashara's reputation and protecting refugees' identities are distinct motives, not proof that every concealment was necessary. |
| Terra and Vokar | Old friends in the life/death cycle. Terra withheld a dying grove from Vokar; he preserved names instead of punishing her. | Keeping a grove green did not make its loss unreal. Vokar's mercy is not proof that concealment was harmless or that grief has one correct timetable. |
| Varun, Dravak and Terra | Varun and Dravak are sworn brothers. Terra sheltered Varun's pack, while evacuation and holding the line divided the brothers' burdens. | Survival does not purchase another person's consent or forgiveness. Refugees are not obliged to act as evidence on either brother's behalf. |
| Nerai and Cryonis | Sisters. Cryonis froze a drowned sanctuary to preserve its final memories; Nerai fears imprisonment. | Preserved memory is not the same as a saved life or an imprisoned conscious soul. Neither sister establishes a complete casualty count or who was alive when the ice closed. |
| Voltira and Zephra | Siblings. Voltira was bound to the Concord; Zephra broke restraints to evacuate mortals, transferring strain to the remaining defense. | Each believes the other abandoned them. Silence, an unsent signal or a flare does not establish intent. Rescue and transferred harm both remain part of the account. |
| Grond and Mechanos | Former mentor and apprentice. Grond built foundations; Mechanos trusted calculation over the maker's warning. Their local failure involved changed support conditions and withheld knowledge. | The district's failure is not a new sole origin of the Sundering. Machines saved lives too. Neither authority nor a calculation supplies a complete account of the workers' fate. |
| Lumen, Ignar and Vokar | Ignar's fire destroyed a refuge, and Ignar secretly helped Lumen preserve its last light. Lumen holds names entrusted by Vokar. Grond and Ignar are old forge companions. | Genuine help does not undo destruction or compel forgiveness. Names are remembrance, not currency. Vokar's entrusted names are not all declared victims of that fire. |

The element group's supporting local account includes Nerai diverting an evacuation current that contributed to flooding, uncertain receipt of Zephra's return gust, and Grond's sealed passage. Keep their scope local and their limits explicit: none supplies a single global cause, a complete roll of the dead, or evidence of a hidden mastermind. The dying grove, drowned sanctuary, failed district and burned refuge are not all asserted to be the same place or simultaneous incident.

## Nine Optional Relationships

The listed order matters: the first letter of `aa`/`ab`/`ba`/`bb` belongs to the first story, the second to the second story. All IDs here have namespace `divinity_engine` and use the existing same-named god stories.

| Relationship | Ordered Gods | Continuing Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dawn_dream` | Solarius, Nythea | Can protection acknowledge the rescue it misread and the survivors it could not recover? |
| `stars_time` | Aethon, Kairoth | Can refusal, warning and scrutiny coexist without spending innocent lives or hiding their cost? |
| `serpent_archive` | Nashara, Elaris | What does a rescuer or keeper of evidence owe those excluded from the truth? |
| `tide_winter` | Nerai, Cryonis | How can preservation answer to loss without calling all custody salvation or imprisonment? |
| `storm_horizon` | Voltira, Zephra | How can freedom and shelter account for the burden transferred to each other? |
| `stone_machine` | Grond, Mechanos | Can warnings, calculations and workers' limits become inspectable rather than authoritative excuses? |
| `root_farewell` | Terra, Vokar | Can care permit change and departure without erasing memory? |
| `pack_valor` | Varun, Dravak | Can protection respect informed limits rather than presume another person's sacrifice? |
| `light_ember` | Lumen, Ignar | Can repair remain possible without buying forgiveness or denying destruction? |

After discovery of both gods, the player sees only the relationship entry title and a generic god-pair teaser. Detailed relation/summary text remains private until both prerequisite stories are complete, or the player already has an earned epilogue for that relationship. This distinction matters because those summaries contain historical facts revealed later in the individual chapter arcs; discovery is not permission to disclose them.

Each new epilogue still requires both independent stories complete and an explicit conversation at an appropriate shrine. Completion may reveal the detailed relationship context but must not automatically deliver or claim the epilogue. The four combinations recognize different practices without adding rewards, favor penalties, compulsory allegiance or a new historical truth that makes the other endings false.

## Authoring Boundaries

Each god's independent story must disclose enough of its own responsibility to stand alone. References to other gods are context, not a requirement to recruit them as witnesses. Preserve the difference between the gods' statements and independently established events; a speaker's fear must not quietly become the narrator's verdict.

Keep each story's stable ID bound to the same god when revising its telling. Runtime reload rejects rebinding an existing story ID to another god to protect persisted identity; new bindings require new story IDs rather than repurposing old player memories.

Remembered cooks, workers, shield bearers, kitchens, lamps, altered songs, records and unnamed survivors give the history specificity. They are not new discoverable structures, actors, collectible proof, recipes or quest items. Narration may leave a casualty count or motive uncertain without hiding the shared canonical anchors behind an optional crossover.

The Vesper stories under `examples/connected-pantheon-*` are explicitly illustrative addon fiction. Vesper's unfinished map, keeper and Solarius conversation do not insert her into the Concord's canonical events or explain the Sundering. Her optional relationship demonstrates the same format as the nine built-ins, not a newly approved tenth canonical relationship.

This document summarizes authored sources, not in-game verification. It makes no claim of Gradle, Forge, persistence, localization or gameplay tests passing.
